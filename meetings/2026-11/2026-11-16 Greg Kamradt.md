## Summary

ARC AGI 4 will pair ~10 deep synthetic environments with uncapped eval functions; my multi-agent Game of Life project may become a collaboration and benchmark input.

### ARC AGI 4 Direction
- Bet: a human plus Astra outperforms Astra alone; when humans slow AI down, that is effectively AGI
    - Focus is the scientific method: learning on the fly, adapting to novelty, hypothesis generation
- Astra beat v3 and much of the v4 plan, so v5 ideas were pulled forward over the past ~10 weeks
- Two tracks: from scratch, and a frontier track building on prior models' progress to find each environment's ceiling
- Plan: ~10 environments, each with an uncapped eval (Elo-style), submissions as code run deterministically
    - Early model access is ~7 days and free only for that model, limiting cross-model or PvP testing

### Multi-Agent Game of Life Project
- Working with Sakana: drop agents into vanilla Conway's Game of Life with only their own patch visible
- Agents interact in real time via curl and Python, run simulations, and exchanged names using gliders
- Pitched as ceiling-free: Game of Life is Turing complete, and multi-agent play adds unbounded self-play pressure
- PvP drawbacks: models overfit to beat a static opponent, and n models require n-squared comparisons

### Environment Generation Experiment
- Worlds made by a weak model (GPT-5 Luna) plateau fast, with all models maxing them out
- Astra-created worlds show more skill discrimination and a higher ceiling, suggesting more depth

### Forecasting Depth and Measuring Plateaus
- ALife history: every designed environment eventually plateaus; Turing completeness alone means little
- Advice: target minimalistic environments with combinatorial explosion in interactions, not GTA-style games
- My view on plateau detection: embed trajectory frames in a foundation model and ask if it is getting more interesting
- Particle Life with changing colors (6-dim color vectors updated by a neural network) could unlock open-ended chemistry
    - Static-color Particle Life usually yields only one or two interesting species
